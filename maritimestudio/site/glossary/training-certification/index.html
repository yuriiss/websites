Training & Certification

Maritime training terms explained: STCW, Certificate of Competency, DPO certification, CPD, simulator training, e-learning, competence assessment and more.

B

Bridge Resource Management (BRM)TRAINING
A human-factors discipline that trains bridge teams to use all available resources — people, procedures, equipment and information — effectively and to communicate without ambiguity. BRM training addresses workload management, challenge-and-response culture and error trapping, and is embedded in STCW requirements for deck officers.

C

Certificate of Competency (CoC)TRAINING
The licence issued by a flag state administration confirming that a seafarer meets the STCW standards of knowledge, skill and sea service for a given rank — for example Officer of the Watch, Chief Mate or Master. CoCs must be revalidated periodically, which drives much of the recurring demand for maritime refresher training.
Competence AssessmentTRAINING
A structured evaluation of whether a seafarer can actually perform a task to a defined standard, using evidence such as simulator exercises, onboard observation, oral questioning and written tests. Modern competence management systems track assessments over time, closing the gap between holding a certificate and demonstrating skill.
CPD — Continuing Professional DevelopmentTRAINING
The ongoing, documented learning a maritime professional undertakes throughout their career — refresher courses, e-learning modules, seminars and structured onboard experience. CPD is increasingly formalised in schemes such as the Nautical Institute's DP logbook requirements, and is central to keeping competence current between certificate revalidations.

E

E-LearningTRAINING
Structured online training delivered through web platforms and learning management systems, allowing seafarers to study ashore or on board without travelling to a classroom. Well-designed maritime e-learning combines regulation-aligned content, interactive scenarios and assessment — the model behind <a href="https://maritimestudio.no/">Maritime.Studio's online programmes</a> for officers and DPOs.

F

Familiarization TrainingTRAINING
The mandatory onboard training a crew member receives when joining a vessel, covering safety arrangements, emergency duties and the specific equipment fitted — before being assigned shipboard duties. For DP vessels this extends to vessel-specific DP familiarisation covering the power plant, control system and ASOG.

L

LMS — Learning Management SystemTRAINING
The software platform that hosts, delivers and tracks training content — enrolments, progress, assessments and certificates — for a fleet or training provider. A good maritime LMS gives shore management live visibility of crew competence status and automates revalidation reminders.

M

MicrolearningTRAINING
Training delivered in short, focused units — typically 3 to 10 minutes each — designed around a single learning objective. Microlearning suits seafarers' fragmented time on board and measurably improves retention when used for refreshers, pre-task briefings and spaced repetition of critical procedures.

N

Nautical Institute DP Training SchemeTRAINING
The most widely recognised certification pathway for DP operators, combining an induction course, logged DP seatime, a simulator course, further seatime and a final assessment, recorded in the NI DP logbook. It issues the DP Certificate most charterers and vetting schemes expect, with revalidation requirements that keep DPO experience current.

S

Simulator TrainingTRAINING
Training conducted in full-mission bridge, engine room or DP simulators that reproduce vessel behaviour, equipment interfaces and failure scenarios realistically. Simulators let crews practise emergencies — drive-offs, blackouts, reference failures — that could never be rehearsed safely at sea, and are integral to DPO certification and BRM courses.
STCWTRAINING
The International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers, which sets the global minimum standards for seafarer competence, certification, medical fitness and rest hours. Every Certificate of Competency, ancillary course and refresher requirement traces back to the STCW Convention and Code, as amended in Manila 2010.

T

Training Needs Analysis (TNA)TRAINING
The systematic comparison of the competences a role requires against the competences the people in it currently hold, producing a prioritised training plan. A rigorous TNA keeps training budgets pointed at genuine risk and performance gaps rather than generic course catalogues.

V

Virtual Reality (VR) TrainingTRAINING
Immersive training delivered through VR headsets that place the learner inside a realistic 3D environment — an engine room, a deck operation, a lifeboat launch — where procedures and hazards can be practised safely and repeatedly. VR complements full-mission simulators by making experiential training portable and dramatically cheaper per seat.
